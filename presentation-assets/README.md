# "Let's Make a Game!" — presentation assets for *The Quest for a Stolen Scepter*

Every image here is either a frame the real game drew, or a sheet made from the
game's own art files. Nothing was painted over, upscaled with AI, or mocked up.

## How these were made (worth one slide on its own)

- The game ships with a **headless play-tester**: it runs the real game with no
  window, feeds it scripted key presses, and saves any frame as a PNG. All game
  screenshots and the video come from **one scripted play session** (seed 1,
  Rowan the Knight, Normal difficulty). Run it again and you get the same
  pixels.
- A small helper "drove" that session like a player: it walked the hero to
  each destination and took the screenshots along the way. Every chest, battle and
  signpost along the route happened by itself; none of it was arranged.
- Screenshots are **2560×1600** (16:10, the game's own shape). The game is laid
  out on a 320×200 grid like a 1990s DOS game, and its art is drawn at 4× that.
  The images are an exact 2× of that art (every art pixel becomes a 2×2 block),
  so nothing is blurred or invented.
- The art is AI-generated on a local machine (Stable Diffusion XL, CC0), the
  music is CC0 from OpenGameArt, and the sound effects are generated by code.
  The game rules are a Rust port of OpenBounty, an open-source reimplementation
  of the 1990 classic *King's Bounty*.

## Details per image

### 01-world-map.png
- **Visible:** Highmoor from above: green land, grey mountain ranges, dark
  forest, sea. Coloured dots mark castles, towns and wandering armies.
- **Concept:** The world is a 64×64 grid; this map is drawn from the same grid.
- **Talking point:** "Every pixel on this map is one step the player can take.
  It sounds small, but you can explore it for hours."
- **Limitation:** Normally fog of war hides unexplored land, and early in this
  session only a sliver of it was uncovered. This picture was taken after the game's built-in
  developer menu (F10 → O) removed the fog. A player who has explored
  everything sees the same thing. The map and the dots are real.

### 01b-overworld-start.png
- **Visible:** The knight on horseback just outside the King's castle, a
  chest, a lake, mountains, an archer band at the bottom left.
- **Talking point:** "In the first second the screen already asks three
  questions: what's in the chest, who is that archer, what's in the castle?"

### 02 / 02b / 02c / 02d — tiles
- **Visible:** Base terrains; the same tiles laid out from the real map file;
  the edge pieces that blend them; the objects.
- **Concept:** Tile-based worlds. About 70 small images produce a whole
  continent. The map file is just 64 lines of letters, one letter per cell.
- **Talking point:** "Lego for landscapes."
- **Limitation:** `02b` shows the terrain only (no castles or creatures) and is
  drawn at half the art size so it fits one slide. The game draws it the same
  way.

### 03 / 03b / 03c / 03d — characters
- **Talking point:** "25 troop types × 4 frames = 100 images, before you draw a
  single villain."
- **Limitation:** There is no "original vs remastered" for troops: their art was
  replaced before the git history used in `10-*` begins. The before/after
  comparisons use terrain, objects, hero and boat instead.

### 04-movement.png → 04b-movement-after.png
- **Visible:** Two steps along a coast. Water and forest block the way. A
  zombie and a fairy (a hostile "sprite") follow the hero; the burning hut is
  a troop dwelling.
- **Concept:** Grid movement, terrain blocking, enemies that chase.
- **Talking point:** "Rules you can see: you can't ride into the sea, and
  monsters walk toward you."

### 05-exploration.png (+ b, c, d)
- **Visible:** "Beneath an old stone you find a buried hoard. A) Keep the 200
  gold. B) Share it…, which raises your leadership by 4." Also a signpost
  ("Castles hold armies. Count them before you knock."), a town's wanted
  notice (bounty 5000 gold) and the "Lay siege (y/n)?" prompt at Castle
  Ironwatch.
- **Talking point:** "Treasure is a decision, not just a reward. Money now, or
  a bigger army forever?"

### 06-combat.png (+ b–e)
- **Visible:** Siege of Castle Ironwatch: the player's archers, pikemen and
  militia (coming in from the gate at the bottom) against orc archers,
  barbarians, skeletons and elves. The number under each figure is how many
  soldiers are left in that group.
- **Concept:** Turn-based tactics: movement points, ranged units, morale.
- **Talking point:** "Same rules, different board: walls change the whole fight."
- **Story note:** The player loses this siege (`06e`): "Beaten in battle, you
  are carried back to the Royal Hall…" The game never let us down gently, and
  that's a good line for the talk: **failure is content.**

### 07-recruitment.png (+ b–d)
- **Visible:** Royal Hall recruitment: Militia 50, Archers 250, Pikemen 300;
  Cavalry/Knights "n/a"; GP=7K; "Max=10": leadership caps how many you may
  lead. After buying, gold drops 7500 → 2500. The town menu prices a boat
  (500/week) and siege engines (3000). The army screen shows weekly upkeep.
- **Talking point:** "Every coin spent here is a coin missing for siege
  engines. Here's the trade-off."

### 08-interface.png (+ b–e)
- **Visible:** Character sheet; contract card (villain portrait, bounty, last
  seen); town gossip about who holds a castle; puzzle map (each villain caught
  removes a piece, revealing where the scepter is buried); controls list.
- **Talking point:** "UI answers the player's questions: who am I, what am I
  doing, where do I go next?"

### 09-gameplay.png / 09b
- **Visible:** Overworld with a castle, forest edge, mountains, a chest and a
  flying sprite following the hero.

### 10-polish-comparison.png (+ b, c, d)
- **Visible:** One starting scene rendered from four git revisions of the art
  (22–24 Oct 2026): placeholder pixel art → painted tiles and hero → re-shaped
  continents with mountain walls and shores → painted frame and sidebar.
- **How:** The current game was run against the older versions of its own
  content pack, taken straight from git. These are real renders, not mock-ups.
- **Talking point:** "Ship ugly first. The rules didn't change in these three
  days; the art passes did."
- **Limitation:** In stage 1 the map itself is older, so the lake sits in a
  slightly different place.

## Limitations, in one place

- **Fully revealed world map** uses the developer menu (disclosed above).
  Nothing else does.
- **No troop before/after**: troop art predates the art history in git.
- **Video has no sound** (headless capture). The game has music
  (`packs/stolen-scepter/audio`): add it in the editor if you want.
- **Resolution**: screenshots are 2560×1600 (16:10). On a 16:9 slide, fit by
  height, or crop top and bottom slightly. Do not stretch.
- **One visible render glitch** in `09-gameplay.png`: thin dark strips along
  the bottom and top edge of the map view, where the forest tile meets the
  frame. It's what the game really draws; nothing was retouched.

## Ideas for the talk

- **Live determinism demo:** the same script always produces the same battle.
  That's how this whole asset set was made, and it's how the engine is tested
  (25 "golden" battle replays must match OpenBounty's C engine to the last digit).
- **The defeat screen** is a nicer story than a victory: the King "sighs,
  lectures you on tactics, then grants you a fresh commission."
- **"About 70 images make a continent"** (`02` → `02b`) lands well with
  people who don't make games.
- **Four art passes in three days** (`10`) shows that a placeholder version
  that plays is worth more than beautiful art that doesn't.
